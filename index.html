About Research Commentary

I study how firms innovate and compete, and how competition law comes to know them.

Postdoctoral Researcher, Department of Law, European University Institute
Research Fellow, Tusher Strategic Initiative for Technology Leadership, Berkeley Haas
Research Fellow, Dynamic Competition Initiative

I am a legal scholar working at the intersection of competition law, empirical law and economics, and the management literature on firm capabilities and innovation. My work asks what competition law would look like if it took seriously how firms actually build, combine, and lose the capabilities that let them innovate.

I completed my PhD in Law at the European University Institute in Florence under the supervision of Nicolas Petit, and joined the Department of Law as a Postdoctoral Researcher in 2026. I was an International Scholar-in-Residence at Georgetown Law in 2024, and before the PhD I practised competition and regulatory law as an associate at Dentons in Istanbul.

At the Dynamic Competition Initiative I lead the capabilities research track and organise the annual young scholars conference. I am Managing Editor of the Journal of Dynamic Competition and co-editor, with Fischer and Hornkohl, of Innovation and the Revamping of Competition Law (Nomos, forthcoming 2026).

Research

My research programme has two limbs that feed each other. One is empirical and looks outward at markets; the other is theoretical and looks inward at the law.

Empirical

How do firms operate as actors of innovation in markets, and what can competition law do about it?

Large-sample studies of merger enforcement and acquisition outcomes, using tools from management research and industrial organisation to test what actually happens to targets, talent, and search after a deal.

Theoretical

How does law receive knowledge about firms and markets, and rebuild it in its own language and logic?

Work on the epistemology of competition law: how concepts such as competition, the firm, and innovation travel from economics and management into legal reasoning, and what gets lost or added on the way.
